## Overview

This repository will host the official implementation of **“Can Information Manipulation Improve Interaction Payoff? A Game-Theoretic Lane-Changing Decision Strategy with eHMI.”**

The project studies how external Human–Machine Interfaces (**eHMIs**) can influence interaction payoffs in lane-changing scenarios. We formulate the interaction between an automated vehicle and surrounding human-driven vehicles from a game-theoretic perspective, and investigate whether information manipulation through eHMI can improve decision quality, interaction efficiency, and overall payoff.

> **Repository status:** The code, experiments, and documentation will be released soon.

---

## Key Ideas

- **Game-theoretic lane-changing decision-making**  
  Models the strategic interaction between the automated vehicle and surrounding traffic participants.

- **eHMI-based information manipulation**  
  Uses external communication signals to influence the belief, response, or payoff structure of other road users.

- **Interaction payoff optimization**  
  Evaluates whether manipulated or selectively communicated information can improve lane-changing outcomes.

- **Human–vehicle interaction perspective**  
  Focuses on how automated vehicles can coordinate with human drivers in mixed traffic environments.

---

## Method at a Glance

The framework will include the following components:

1. **Scenario construction**  
   Lane-changing interaction scenarios involving an automated vehicle and neighboring human-driven vehicles.

2. **Game-theoretic modeling**  
   A strategic decision model for capturing interaction payoff under different information conditions.

3. **eHMI strategy design**  
   Information display or manipulation strategies used to shape the interaction process.

4. **Decision and payoff evaluation**  
   Quantitative analysis of safety, efficiency, and interaction payoff under different strategies.
